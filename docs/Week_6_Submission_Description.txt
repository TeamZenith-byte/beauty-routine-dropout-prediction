This is my Week 6 submission for the YuvaIntern Data Science with Python Analyst Internship, the final task of the project Beauty Routine Dropout Prediction: Identifying Why Customers Abandon Multi-Step Skincare Routines. The report designs how the three Week 5 models (Logistic Regression as the primary baseline, Decision Tree as the interpretable comparison and Random Forest as the ensemble comparison) will be evaluated and how the findings will be communicated to technical and non-technical readers.

The report explains Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC with formulas, skincare examples and reasons for choosing each, and explains TP, TN, FP, FN, the confusion matrix, the classification threshold, cross-validation, stratified sampling, overfitting, underfitting and data leakage. It sets out a validation plan with a locked test set, stratified and group-aware nested cross-validation, nested out-of-fold threshold selection and leakage checks, gives a cost-based way to choose the threshold, and provides blank comparison tables, a workflow diagram, a validation flowchart, labelled illustrative ROC and PR schematics, a business impact workflow and a final recommendation framework. It also gives conditional recommendations for marketing, product, customer support and decision-makers.

I am clear about the limits. There are no verified real customer routine_dropout labels, the public Sephora and Open Beauty Facts data are only proxy sources, the Week 3 pipeline and the Week 5 and Week 6 Python templates were tested only on simulated data, the BRFS weights are unvalidated and the hypotheses H1 to H5 are untested. So this report contains no accuracy, ROC-AUC or other performance values, no model ranking and no business-impact figures, and it is not a completed real-world evaluation. Part A lists the work actually done and Part B describes how evaluation and deployment would proceed once verified labelled data are collected through a future survey and routine diary.
